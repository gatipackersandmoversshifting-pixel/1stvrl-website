Shifting in Talwara: what we plan for

Talwara owes its present shape to the Beas project. The township was built for the engineers and staff of the Pong Dam, which holds back the Beas just across the Himachal border, and of the Shah Nehar barrage downstream, where the Mukerian Hydel Channel begins. The Bhakra Beas Management Board still runs much of the township, as a planned township of quarters, schools and wide, shaded roads, and BBMB postings bring a steady stream of transfers in and out. Beyond the township lie the older villages and small towns of the Kandi belt, such as Datarpur, and the hill temple of Kamahi Devi, with Hajipur and Mukerian on the road south towards NH44. Many local families have members in the army or living abroad. Houses are a mix of government quarters, independent homes and farm houses on sloping ground. Summers are hot, the monsoon is heavy and can bring landslips on hill roads, and winters are cold and foggy in the mornings.

Monsoon and winter on the hill roads

From July to September rain is heavy at the foot of the Shivaliks, seasonal streams can rise fast, and hill roads sometimes see landslips. The coordinator checks the road the evening before and keeps a spare date in hand.

Mattresses go into corrugated sheet and film, and every carton is taped on all seams. In December and January fog on the road towards Mukerian and NH44 can last into the morning, so long-distance trucks leave once it clears.
