Shifting in Shahabad Markanda: what we plan for

Shahabad Markanda is a subdivision town of Kurukshetra district, strung along NH44 with Ambala to the north and Pipli and Thanesar to the south. The town takes the second half of its name from the Markanda, a Shivalik river that is a quiet, sandy bed for most of the year and a fast brown flood in a heavy monsoon. Fields of paddy, wheat and sugarcane surround the town, the grain market fills twice a year, and the cooperative sugar mill draws cane from the villages every winter. Shahabad is also known across Haryana for women's hockey, and many players from its grounds have gone on to play for India. The railway station sits on the main Ambala to Delhi line. Homes are mostly independent houses: older double-storey buildings in the bazaar, and newer kothis and plotted colonies off the highway and the Ladwa road.

When the Markanda rises

The river that gives the town its name can change the plan for a July or August move. After days of heavy rain in the Shivalik hills the Markanda can rise quickly, and in bad years it has spilled into low-lying colonies and fields near its banks. If your house is on the river side, the coordinator checks the water level and the roads the evening before loading.

Monsoon packing is done with the rain in mind. Mattresses, sofas and wooden furniture get a plastic layer under the blankets, cartons are kept off wet floors on the truck, and electronics are sealed in their own boxes. We keep a spare day in the plan, because a flooded approach road is not worth the risk to your goods.

Cane trolleys, the sugar mill and harvest weeks

From about November to March the cooperative sugar mill crushes cane, and tractor trolleys piled high and wide queue on the roads leading to it. In April the wheat harvest fills the grain market, and in October and November paddy does the same. A household truck stuck behind those trolleys on a narrow road can lose hours.

If your house is near the mill or the mandi, tell us at the survey. The crew can load at first light, before the trolleys start rolling, and the driver takes the quickest way onto NH44. Families who farm can also pick a date just after the crop is sold, when both the roads and their own time are freer.
