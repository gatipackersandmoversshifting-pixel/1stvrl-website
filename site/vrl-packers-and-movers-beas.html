Shifting in Beas: what we plan for

Beas lies on the Amritsar side of the Beas river, where the GT Road (the old NH 1) crosses the bridge into Kapurthala district. Administratively it falls in Baba Bakala tehsil of Amritsar district, and much of the town sits within the revenue limits of Budha Theh, with parts in Dholo Nangal and Wazir Bhullar. Beas Junction railway station, on Dera Road, is where the Beas to Tarn Taran branch leaves the Ambala to Attari main line. Just north of the town is the headquarters of Radha Soami Satsang Beas, also known as Dera Baba Jaimal Singh, and the Maharaj Sawan Singh Charitable Hospital stands on the GT Road. Housing ranges from shops with flats above them along the highway to independent houses and newer colonies off Dera Road. Amritsar airport is roughly 51 km away. The river brings humid summers and heavy monsoon rain, and winter fog often sits low over the bridge.

Which side of the river you live on matters

Beas and Budha Theh are in Amritsar district, but the far bank of the river, from the bridge towards Dhilwan, is Kapurthala district. A move from Beas to Baba Bakala or Rayya side villages stays local when it is within about 30 km, while a move across the bridge to Kapurthala or Jalandhar is in a different district and goes on the up to 500 km band.

For that reason the survey notes your exact pickup and drop addresses, not just the town names. A 1 BHK from Beas to Kapurthala, for example, is roughly Rs 9,500 to 17,000 plus GST, while a 1 BHK to Baba Bakala is roughly Rs 5,000 to 9,000 plus GST.

The GT Road through Beas carries heavy truck and bus traffic all day, and many homes and shops open straight onto it. The partner team parks the truck on the side lane or a nearby open plot where possible, so cartons are not carried across the main carriageway.
