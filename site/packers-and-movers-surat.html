Shifting in Surat: what we plan for

Surat grew up on both banks of the Tapi, and that river shapes almost every house move in the city. On the western side, Adajan, Pal, Rander, Jahangirpura and the newer belt towards Palanpur and the Hazira road are full of tall residential towers built in the last twenty years. South of the old city, the Athwa Lines, Ghod Dod Road, City Light, Piplod, Vesu and Althan stretch along Dumas Road and the airport side, with large flats and many bungalows. East of the railway line, Varachha, Mota Varachha, Katargam, Sarthana and Puna are home to the diamond trade and to thousands of families who came from Saurashtra, while Udhna, Pandesara, Limbayat and Dindoli carry the textile mills, dyeing houses and workers' housing. The old walled city around Chowk, Nanpura, Gopipura and Mahidharpura has narrow lanes and tall old houses. The weather is hot and humid most of the year, and the south-west monsoon from late June to September brings heavy rain off the Arabian Sea.

The first thing the partner team checks at the survey is the route from the building to the road and, for cross-river moves, which bridge the truck will use. Towers in Adajan, Pal and Vesu usually have a service lift, a security desk and a society office that asks for a move date in advance, so the coordinator fixes that slot before the truck is booked. In Varachha and Katargam, many homes sit above shops or workshops on busy roads, and the crew loads early in the morning before the markets and the diamond units fill the street. The old city lanes near Gopipura and Nanpura are often too narrow for a large truck, so goods are carried to a small tempo and moved out to the main road. Metro construction has narrowed parts of several main roads, and goods vehicles face timing limits in busy areas, which is why most loading in the centre is planned at dawn or after dark.

Packers and movers in Adajan, Vesu and the Dumas Road side

Packers and movers in Adajan and Pal spend most of their time in high-rise societies: a 2 BHK or 3 BHK on the twelfth or fifteenth floor, a shared service lift and a guard who checks every carton against the gate pass. The crew pads the lift walls, books it for a fixed block of hours and brings trolleys for the long walk from the tower lobby to the truck. Rander and Jahangirpura, just north along the river, mix older houses with new towers, and the lanes near Rander's old core can be tight enough that the truck waits on the main road.

Vesu, Piplod, Althan and the colonies along Dumas Road and VIP Road have some of the largest homes in Surat, with big wardrobes, glass dining tables, swing chairs and air conditioners in every room. The work here is mostly about careful dismantling and wrapping rather than access. Families moving from these areas often have staff quarters, a home office or a puja room with brass and silver items, and the surveyor lists those separately so they are packed by hand and opened first at the new address.

Diamond, textile and Hazira moves: offices and staff transfers

Surat's two big trades create a steady stream of office moves. Diamond firms shift between the older units of Varachha, Mahidharpura and Katargam and the newer office space at Khajod, around the Surat Diamond Bourse, and each move involves safes, polishing tables, computers and cabinets full of records. Textile traders on Ring Road and in the markets near Salabatpura and Sahara Darwaja move showrooms, sample racks and accounts offices. A small office of up to 10 workstations inside the city is about Rs 12,000 to 25,000 plus GST on the local band, and a medium office of 10 to 30 workstations about Rs 25,000 to 60,000 plus GST.

Hazira adds an industrial group. Engineers and staff at the steel, shipbuilding, gas, fertiliser and petrochemical units there often live in company townships or in Adajan and Pal, and transfers to Mumbai, Pune, Delhi or other plants are common. When the employer reimburses the move, the invoice can carry the company name and GSTIN, with the signed inventory and lorry receipt attached. Bank and PSU staff whose claim needs an IBA-approved mover are moved through our IBA-approved partner (code MUK-2068). Students and faculty at SVNIT and Veer Narmad South Gujarat University, on the Ichchhanath and Udhna-Magdalla side, usually need a single room moved on the 1 RK row.

Monsoon, humidity and the river: planning home shifting in Surat

Home shifting in Surat during the monsoon needs a little extra planning. Heavy spells between July and September can leave water standing on low roads and near the creeks for some hours, and the Tapi rises after rain in its upper catchment. The crew wraps sofas and mattresses in plastic, double-tapes every carton and keeps the truck covered with a tarpaulin, and the coordinator is ready to move the date by a day if a spell of very heavy rain is forecast. Because the air stays damp, wooden furniture is wrapped only on the day of loading, so moisture is not sealed in for long.

From March to June the heat is the main problem, with humid afternoons that tire a crew carrying cartons down fifteen floors. Loading starts early, the fridge and electronics stay in the shade until the truck is ready, and nothing that melts, such as candles or cosmetics, should go in the truck. November to February is the most comfortable season for a move. Diwali is Surat's biggest holiday: many diamond and textile units close for a long break, families travel to their home towns and labour is short, so a move around that period is best booked well ahead.
