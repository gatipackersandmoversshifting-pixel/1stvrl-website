Shifting in Kosli: what we plan for

Kosli belongs to the Ahirwal belt of southern Haryana, a region with a long and proud record of army service, and that shows in the town: many families have a serving soldier or a pensioner, and moves after a posting or retirement are common. The town is the headquarters of a tehsil and gives its name to an assembly seat, and its bazaar, grain market and bus stand serve a wide spread of villages towards Nahar, Jatusana and the Jhajjar side, including large villages such as Lula Ahir. The land is sandy and dry, and farming depends on canals and tube wells, with bajra, mustard and wheat the main crops. Houses are mostly independent, from older homes on narrow lanes behind the bazaar to newer houses with a gate and a front yard on the roads out of town. Many younger people commute to or work in Rewari, Gurugram, Bawal and Delhi, and a move often means setting up a family in an NCR flat. Summers are long and very hot, with loo winds in May and June, and the short winters are cold with fog on the morning roads.

Hot summers, loo and dust

From late April the afternoons around Kosli are harsh, and the hot loo wind carries sand and dust. Loading is planned for the early morning, cartons are taped on every seam, and electronics travel in padded cartons stacked away from the rear doors of the truck.
