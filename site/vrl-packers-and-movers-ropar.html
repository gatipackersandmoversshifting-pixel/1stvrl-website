Shifting in Ropar: what we plan for

Ropar is a district town on the banks of the Sutlej, at the point where the river leaves the Shivalik hills for the Punjab plains. The older town around the main bazaar has close-packed houses and lanes that a full-size truck cannot enter, while newer homes have come up along the Chandigarh road and in colonies such as Gyani Zail Singh Nagar. The IIT Ropar campus on the edge of town and the thermal power plant at Ghanauli bring faculty, students, engineers and their families in and out every year. The highway from Kharar through Ropar towards Kiratpur Sahib and the hills carries heavy traffic, so we fix where the truck will stand before the day.

Faculty, students and plant staff

IIT Ropar draws faculty and research staff from across the country, and many arrive or leave with a full household and a car. Students finishing a degree usually need only a few cartons and a cycle or a bike sent home. We list books, instruments and study furniture room by room at the survey, and each vehicle is quoted as its own line from the car and bike rate cards.

Engineers and staff at the Ghanauli thermal plant and the units around Ropar often live in colony quarters with a date for handing back the keys. Tell us that date when you book, and the packing is planned so the quarter is empty with a day to spare. The itemised GST invoice shows packing, labour and transport on separate lines for the claim.

Floods, fog and the river side of town

Low-lying parts of Ropar near the Sutlej and the canals can take water after heavy rain in July and August, and the district has seen floods in recent monsoons. In those weeks we check the approach road on the morning of the move, line the truck floor and wrap mattresses and sofas in plastic before they leave the house.

In December and January, dense fog on the roads towards Chandigarh and Ludhiana slows driving after dark. Short moves are loaded and delivered in daylight, and longer runs start early so most of the journey is done before evening.
