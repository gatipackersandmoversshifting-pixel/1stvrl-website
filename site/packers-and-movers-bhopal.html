Shifting in Bhopal: what we plan for

Home shifting in Bhopal means dealing with two very different cities. Old Bhopal, around and north of the Lower Lake, is a maze of bazaars around Chowk, Peer Gate, Ibrahimpura, Budhwara and Itwara, where houses share walls, staircases are steep and a full truck cannot get past the first corner. New Bhopal spreads south and east in planned colonies: New Market and TT Nagar, the E-blocks of Arera Colony, MP Nagar, Shahpura, Char Imli and Shivaji Nagar, then the newer townships of Kolar Road, Awadhpuri, Ayodhya Bypass, Katara Hills and the long belt along Hoshangabad Road out to Misrod. The city is also hilly. Shyamla Hills, Idgah Hills, Arera Hills and Katara Hills all have sloping lanes, and a loaded truck parked on a slope needs wheel chocks and a careful carry down the driveway. Summers are harsh, with May afternoons often above 40 degrees, and most of the year's rain falls in a few heavy weeks of July and August.

At the survey, the partner team first checks how close the truck can get. In the old city lanes around Chowk and Jahangirabad, cartons are carried out by hand or loaded onto a small tempo that shuttles to the main road. In the BHEL township around Piplani and Barkheda, and in the government quarters of TT Nagar and Char Imli, most homes are ground or low floors with wide doors but long walks to the road, so trolleys come along. Newer towers on Kolar Road, Hoshangabad Road and Ayodhya Bypass usually have a lift and a society office that wants notice of the date. Heavy goods vehicles face daytime limits on several busy city roads, so loading in the central areas is often planned for early morning or late evening.

Packers and movers in Arera Colony, Kolar Road and the lake side

Packers and movers in Arera Colony and Shahpura mostly handle large independent houses and bungalows with gardens, old teak furniture and heavy almirahs, and the work is slower because pieces are big rather than because access is poor. Around New Market, TT Nagar and South TT Nagar the homes are older and many are government allotments, so the surveyor asks early about any handover inspection the department needs. Along VIP Road and Shyamla Hills, on the edge of the Upper Lake, houses sit on slopes with tight turns, and the truck is often parked on the main road while a smaller vehicle does the last stretch.

Kolar Road has grown fast, with apartment towers, row houses and plotted colonies such as Chuna Bhatti, Sarvadharam and Nayapura, and on weekends its single main road gets crowded. Packers and movers in Kolar Road therefore prefer weekday mornings for loading. Awadhpuri, Saket Nagar and the colonies near AIIMS draw many doctors, nurses and resident staff who move between rented flats every year or two, while Katara Hills and Bagmugalia on the Hoshangabad Road side have newer societies with lifts and gate registers.

Weather, slopes and old city lanes: planning home shifting in Bhopal

May and early June are the hardest months to load a truck in Bhopal. The crew starts at first light, keeps wooden pieces and the fridge in shade while the vehicle is filled and avoids leaving a loaded truck standing in the afternoon sun. In July and August, heavy rain can come in long spells and some low roads and underpasses collect water, so cartons are taped twice, mattresses and sofas are wrapped in plastic and the date is kept flexible by a day where possible. October to February is the easiest season, with dry roads and mild days.

Slopes need their own plan. In Shyamla Hills, Idgah Hills, Koh-e-Fiza and parts of Katara Hills, the truck is parked on the flattest stretch with its wheels chocked, and heavy items such as almirahs and washing machines are carried down on straps rather than rolled. In the old city near Peer Gate and Moti Masjid, the partner team checks lane width and staircase turns during the survey, and large beds or sofas are opened up upstairs. Tell the coordinator about narrow doors or a spiral stair before the date is fixed, so the right number of hands arrives.
