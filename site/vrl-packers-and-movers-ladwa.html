Shifting in Ladwa: what we plan for

Ladwa was once the seat of a small Sikh state that the British annexed in the 1840s, and the old town still has the feel of a market centre that served the villages around it. Today it is a farm town at a road crossing: one road runs to Pipli and Kurukshetra, another to Radaur and Yamunanagar, a third towards Indri and Karnal, and a fourth to Shahabad. Wheat, paddy and sugarcane grow on the surrounding land, and rows of poplar along the fields feed the plywood units of the Yamunanagar belt. Many families farm, trade in grain and fertiliser, or run workshops and transport firms. Homes are mostly independent houses, often with a courtyard and a tractor shed, and newer double-storey kothis stand along the main roads. Summers are hot and humid, and winter fog is common on the Indri and Pipli roads.

Courtyard houses and farm families

A Ladwa farmhouse holds more than its room count suggests. Big wooden diwans, steel trunks of bedding, brass utensils, grain drums and a deep freezer can fill a truck that a city 2 BHK would only half use. During the survey the partner team opens the stores and the upper rooms, not just the drawing room, so the vehicle size and the price are right first time.

Farm tools, diesel cans and gas cylinders are not loaded with household goods, for safety. If something from the shed must go, the coordinator tells you at the survey how it can travel, or advises that it stays behind with the family.

Sugarcane trolleys and the crossroads traffic

During the crushing season, roughly November to March, sugarcane trolleys head out on the roads around Ladwa towards the mills, and they move slowly and wide. Paddy trolleys do the same in October. A truck parked on a narrow road in those weeks can block traffic both ways, so loading is planned for times when the roads are quieter.

The crossroads at the heart of town is the busiest spot of the day, with buses, shoppers and tractors all meeting. Houses and shops near the bazaar are loaded early in the morning, and the team places a helper at the back of the truck to guide traffic around it.

Home shifting in Ladwa for traders and shopkeepers

Many Ladwa families live above or behind their shop. When the household moves, a counter, glass showcases, steel racks or a small safe may need to go to a new shop or be stored with relatives. The estimate lists the shop goods apart from the home, so you can see each cost.

Glass shelves are taken out and wrapped one by one, and racks are taken apart and bundled with their screws taped to a frame. The partner team photographs complicated fittings before dismantling, which makes it easier for you or your own carpenter to put them back at the new address.
