Shifting in Kartarpur: what we plan for

Kartarpur was founded by Guru Arjan Dev, the fifth Sikh Guru, in the late 16th century, and Gurdwara Thamm Sahib stands at the heart of the old town. In 1635 Guru Hargobind's forces fought a battle against the Mughals here. Today the town is known across Punjab for its furniture market: showrooms and workshops along the GT Road sell beds, sofas, dining sets and almirahs to buyers from Jalandhar, Amritsar and far beyond. The Jang-e-Azadi Memorial, which honours Punjab's part in the freedom struggle, stands on the highway at the edge of town. Kartarpur has a railway station on the main line from Ambala to Amritsar. Homes range from old brick houses in the lanes around the gurdwara to newer kothis and colonies along the GT Road and the Kapurthala road, and many residents work in Jalandhar and come home each evening.

New furniture from the Kartarpur market

People often buy a full set of new furniture in Kartarpur and then need it carried to a new home in another city, sometimes together with the rest of the household. New polished wood scratches easily, so each piece is wrapped in soft padding and stretch film, corners get extra protection, and glass tops travel upright in their own crates or cartons.

If new furniture is joining an existing house move, tell the surveyor which pieces will be collected from the showroom, so the truck has space and the route covers both stops. The estimate lists these pieces separately. A 2 BHK plus new furniture to Delhi is priced on the up to 500 km band, where a 2 BHK is roughly Rs 13,000 to 24,000 plus GST, with the extra items added after the survey.

The old town around Gurdwara Thamm Sahib

The lanes around the gurdwara are narrow and busy, especially on gurpurabs and on the days of the town's religious gatherings. A truck can rarely reach the door, so the partner team parks on the nearest open road and carries goods out by hand or on a trolley, and the survey records that carry for the estimate.

Families who have lived near the gurdwara for generations often keep gutkas, rumalas and other items they treat with special respect. Tell the surveyor, and these will be packed separately in clean cartons, kept apart from the general load and handed over first at the new home.
