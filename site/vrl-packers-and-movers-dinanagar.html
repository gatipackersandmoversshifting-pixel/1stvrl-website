Shifting in Dinanagar: what we plan for

Dinanagar sits on the Amritsar to Pathankot national highway and the railway line of the same name, between Gurdaspur and Pathankot and not far from the international border. It was the summer capital of Maharaja Ranjit Singh, who held his court here in May and June, and his Baradari mansion still stands among old mango groves. Today Dinanagar is a sub-division town with conduit pipe units, schools and colleges, and a farming belt known for fine aromatic paddy, though most of the old rice mills have closed. The town has a railway overbridge. The Ravi river flows not far away, and a pontoon bridge, taken out during the monsoon, connects a group of riverside villages to the town. Villages such as Awankha, Behrampur and Behlolpur are part of the surrounding tehsil. Homes range from older houses in the bazaar to newer independent houses near the highway. Summers are hot, the monsoon is heavy, and winter mornings are foggy.

Gurdaspur is the district headquarters and only about 13 km away, so a move between Dinanagar and Gurdaspur is billed on the local band. A 1 BHK is roughly Rs 5,000 to 9,000 plus GST and a 3 BHK roughly Rs 12,000 to 21,000 plus GST.

Pathankot is close too, but it has been a separate district since 2011, so a move from Dinanagar to Pathankot goes on the up to 500 km band. There a 1 BHK is roughly Rs 9,500 to 17,000 plus GST. The written estimate always shows the band and the road distance.

Home shifting in Dinanagar during the monsoon

July to September brings heavy rain to this sub-mountain belt, and the Ravi rises. For home shifting in Dinanagar in those months, the partner team keeps a tarpaulin ready, seals every carton seam, bags mattresses and sofas in plastic, and loads only when the rain eases.
