Shifting in Fatehgarh Churian: what we plan for

Fatehgarh Churian is a municipal council town in Gurdaspur district with a long history. Before 1947 it sat on busy trade routes that linked Amritsar, Gurdaspur and Batala with Sialkot, and its old bazaars still have the feel of that pre-Partition Punjab. Its best-known monument is the Panj Mandir, a temple about two centuries old built during the Khalsa Raj by Chand Kaur of the Kanhaiya Misl, a daughter-in-law of Maharaja Ranjit Singh, and known for its frescoes. The railway station on the Amritsar to Dera Baba Nanak line keeps its colonial-era building. Housing is a mix of old brick houses in narrow bazaar lanes and larger independent homes on the edges of town. Villages such as Sekhwan, Khehra, Chatha, Badowal Kalan, Chichrewal and Kot Khazana belong to the surrounding sub-district. Paddy and wheat fill the fields, summers are hot, and fog is thick in winter.

Old bazaar lanes need a plan

Many of the oldest houses in Fatehgarh Churian face lanes that are too narrow for a full-size truck. The partner team checks this at the survey and, if needed, parks on the wider road and carries goods out on trolleys and by hand, or uses a smaller vehicle for the last stretch.

These houses often have steep, narrow stairs and wooden doors with low frames. Beds and dining tables are opened up, almirahs are emptied before they are moved, and corners are padded so the old walls and door frames are not marked on the way out. Old wooden doors and carved frames in heritage houses are left untouched; if a door has to be taken off its hinges to pass a sofa, it is done only with your consent and put back the same day.
