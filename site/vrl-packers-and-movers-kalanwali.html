Shifting in Kalanwali: what we plan for

Kalanwali, often called Mandi Kalanwali, is a municipal committee town and tehsil headquarters in the north of Sirsa district, right next to the Punjab border. It has a railway station on the line between Bathinda and Sirsa, and its grain and cotton market draws farmers from both sides of the state line, which is why Punjabi is heard here as much as Haryanvi and Hindi. Cotton, wheat and paddy are the main crops, and the market becomes very busy at harvest. The town's homes are mostly independent houses, with traders' houses near the mandi and newer houses on the roads leading out of town. Villages of the tehsil such as Odhan, Rori, Kewal, Ghukanwali, Banwala, Dogranwali and Chormar Khera surround it. Summers in this part of Haryana are very hot and dry, with dust storms in May and June, while winter nights are cold and fog settles over the fields in December and January.

Why Sirsa is not on the local rate

Our same-district rule gives the local band to moves within about 30 km by road. Sirsa is in the same district, but it is roughly 40 km from Kalanwali, so it falls on the up to 500 km band. There a 1 BHK is roughly Rs 9,500 to 17,000 plus GST.

Home shifting in Kalanwali in dust and fog

May and June are fierce here, with afternoon temperatures well above 40 degrees and dust blowing in from dry fields. For home shifting in Kalanwali in summer, the partner team starts early, seals every carton against dust, and keeps the fridge, TV and washing machine wrapped and away from the hot rear doors of the truck.

Winter is the opposite. Fog over the Sirsa and Bathinda roads can last well into the morning, so long-distance trucks leave after it lifts. Your estimate gives a delivery window that allows for slow driving.

Harvest weeks at the mandi

In April and again in October and November, tractor-trolleys and trucks crowd the roads into the Kalanwali mandi. Loading near the market is planned for very early morning, and the truck is parked where it does not block the arrivals.

If your new house is in a village, the survey also checks the approach road, since some village lanes are too narrow for a large truck. A smaller vehicle for the last stretch is arranged in advance and written into the estimate. Cotton picking in autumn also means extra labour moving between villages, so booking a week ahead in that season makes it easier to get the date you want.
