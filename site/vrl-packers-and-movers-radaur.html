Shifting in Radaur: what we plan for

Radaur is a compact sub-division town on the road between Yamunanagar and Ladwa, with a grain market, a main bazaar and a ring of farming villages around it. The land here grows wheat, paddy and sugarcane, and tall rows of poplar along the field edges are cut and sold to the plywood and veneer units of the Yamunanagar and Jagadhri belt. Many households farm, trade in timber or run small transport and workshop businesses, and a good number of men work in the industrial area of Yamunanagar and travel back each evening. Topra Kalan, a village of the Radaur block, is known as the original site of the Ashokan pillar that Firoz Shah Tughlaq carried to Delhi in the 14th century, and a park with a pillar replica now marks the spot. Homes range from old brick houses near the bazaar to newer kothis on the main roads. Summers are hot and humid, the monsoon is heavy, and fog is common from mid-December.

Home shifting in Radaur's farm houses and villages

Village houses around Radaur often have a big courtyard, a cattle shed and rooms added over the years. For home shifting in Radaur's villages the team looks at the gate width, the state of the link road after rain and where a truck can turn. Sugarcane season and the roads to the mills

From about November to April, tractor trolleys stacked with sugarcane move slowly towards the mills of the Yamunanagar area, and the roads out of Radaur get crowded in the mornings. Trucks loaded with poplar logs for the plywood units add to the traffic. The coordinator plans the loading time so the household truck is not stuck behind a long line.

Wheat harvest in April and paddy procurement in October also fill the Radaur grain market with trolleys. If your house is near the mandi, the team aims for early morning loading or picks a day when the auction is quieter, and tells you the plan in advance.

Monsoon rain and winter fog around Radaur

Heavy July and August rain can leave the low fields and some village approach roads waterlogged for a day or two. For moves in those months, upholstered furniture and mattresses are wrapped in plastic sheet under the blankets, and the truck waits on firm ground rather than a soft verge.

In December and January, fog on the Ladwa and Yamunanagar roads can be thick before sunrise. Long-distance trucks leave once visibility improves, and the coordinator gives a delivery window that allows for it rather than a time the road cannot keep.
