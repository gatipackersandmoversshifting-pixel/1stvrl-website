Shifting in Kot Ise Khan: what we plan for

Kot Ise Khan takes its name from Ise Khan Manj, the chief who founded it, and local history records that Jain traders from the Ferozepur side settled here in the sixteenth century, which helped it grow into a trading centre for the surrounding villages. Today it is a municipal town of about 13,000 people in Dharamkot tehsil, set in flat farmland under wheat and paddy. NH703B carries traffic east to Moga and west to Makhu, where it meets NH54 towards Amritsar, and Zira, across the district line in Ferozepur, is close by. The town has a grain market, a main bazaar of shops and commission agents, and a growing ring of newer houses along the Moga and Zira roads. Like much of Moga district, many families here have members working in Canada or Australia, so a move often means partly closing a house rather than emptying it. The monsoon can be heavy, and winter fog settles thick over the fields.
